## How to use

Three outcomes:

- **Prohibited** — say so plainly and stop. Don't soften the product description to slip it through; disguising a prohibited product is a separate violation under deceptive practices.
- **Restricted** — allowed only with preconditions. Collect them into a checklist the advertiser must satisfy before launch, and write required disclaimers into the first draft of the script.
- **Clear** — proceed to the content rules.

Market matters more than category. Several markets prohibit paid ads entirely; others carve out exceptions. **When the market or the category is unstated, ask. Don't assume either one.** Don't default to the US or any other market, and don't infer the market from the user's language or currency. If a product could sit in more than one category, say which one you'd use and get the user to confirm it before applying this file.

## Globally prohibited

Not advertisable anywhere on TikTok, in any format:

- Tobacco and nicotine in all forms — cigarettes, cigars, chewing tobacco, e-cigarettes, vape pens, vape oils and cartridges. (Anti-smoking campaigns and non-pharmaceutical cessation products are allowed.)
- Weapons — guns, gun parts, ammunition, paintball guns; blades, knives, and sharp objects. (Narrow exception: knives may be permitted in select European markets with 18+ targeting.)
- Illegal drugs, controlled substances, recreational drugs, and drug paraphernalia or accessories.
- Prescription medicines, except limited approved cases in Canada, the US, and New Zealand.
- Counterfeit goods — replicas, imitations, and non-genuine products passed off as genuine.
- Get-rich-quick offers, pyramid schemes, and too-good-to-be-true financial offers.
- Bail bonds, credit repair services, penny auctions, pawnbroker loans.
- Clinical trial recruitment; adoption and surrogacy services.
- Elective, reconstructive, and weight-loss surgery in most markets; invasive cosmetic procedures including rhinoplasty, face and lip fillers, and microdermabrasion. (Exceptions since 2026: cosmetic surgery and aesthetic procedures in the US and Canada, and weight-loss surgery in the markets listed under [2026 changes](#market-specific-changes-in-2026).)
- Unlicensed or illegal gambling services.
- Content that discloses personal data or implies a product can be used to violate someone's privacy.

## Healthcare and Pharmaceuticals

Among the highest-rejection verticals. Baseline: **18+ targeting**, plus regulatory approval on file.

**Restricted, requires authorization:**

| Sub-category | Preconditions |
|---|---|
| OTC medicines, medical devices | Proof of approval/certification from local regulators; 18+; devices registered locally with CE or UKCA marking where relevant |
| Cosmetic clinics and aesthetic services | Regulatory approval; 18+; sales-rep approval; no misleading claims. **US and CA (since March 2026):** cosmetic surgery also allowed, with medical risks and recovery times disclosed and no exaggerated before/after. **France:** influencers may not promote cosmetic procedures at all |
| Pharmacies | Licensing certification; **prohibited outright in France, Greece, Japan** |
| Supplements and dietary products | Regulatory approval; 18+; some markets require health-risk disclosure in the local language |
| Condoms and lubricants | 18+; must not focus on sexual pleasure or be overtly sexual |
| Contraceptives | Market-specific; allowed in Sri Lanka since May 2026 with local regulator approval and age targeting |
| Prescription drugs (CA/US/NZ only) | Regulator-approved, medically accurate, age-gated; NZ requires 25+ |
| Infant formula | Prohibited for 0–24 months in most markets (allowed in Azerbaijan since June 2026, 18+); follow-on formula may be allowed with no mention of being a breast-milk substitute |

**Health claims — prohibited in most markets regardless of vertical:**

- Medicinal claims to treat, cure, heal, or prevent any medical condition.
- Any implication that serious diseases — cancer, HIV, COVID-19, MS, Parkinson's — are treatable by the product.
- "Miracle" framing, or claims of equivalence to prescription medicine.
- **Before-and-after comparisons for OTC medicines and supplements.**
- Cosmetics and beauty products making medical claims, unless the advertiser holds an approved medical license or certification.

The practical rewrite: describe ingredients, formulation, and sensory experience rather than physiological outcomes. "Formulated with hyaluronic acid" clears; "clears your eczema in 7 days" does not.

## Weight Management and Body Image

Weight-loss and muscle-gain claims are **restricted to 18+** audiences. Beyond that, the body-image rules apply to *any* vertical showing bodies — fashion, fitness, beauty, food — not just weight-loss products.

**Prohibited:**

- References to unrealistic muscle gain or weight loss.
- Encouraging unhealthy lifestyles or unhealthy relationships with food.
- Claims that the product alone, without diet or exercise, causes weight loss or muscle gain.
- Suggesting that losing weight or gaining muscle is easy or guaranteed.
- Body shaming, explicitly or implicitly.
- Suggesting there is an ideal body type, or a "right" or "perfect" way to look.
- Linking body appearance to improved life circumstances, self-image, desirability, success, happiness, or popularity.
- Promoting or reinforcing negative or unhealthy body images.

Before/after body comparisons are the classic failure here: they simultaneously imply guaranteed results, an ideal body type, and a life-outcome link.

Weight-loss supplements, meal replacements and weight-loss surgery are allowed only in specific markets, with sales-rep approval, age targeting and local licences. Since July 2026, weight-management supplements are allowed across EMEA, North America and LATAM. Since May 2026, the APAC and Middle East/Africa markets listed under [2026 changes](#market-specific-changes-in-2026) also allow surgery and meal replacements. Look up the user's market in that table rather than assuming. In the EU, Reg. (EC) 1924/2006 still bans any rate or amount of weight loss in claims for foods and supplements (see `references/law/eu-eea.md`).

## Financial Services

Universal requirements for anything financial: **licensed by local and regional authorities**, **18+ targeting**, compliance with local law, and proper disclaimers.

**Prohibited:** get-rich-quick offers, pyramid schemes, bail bonds, credit repair, penny auctions, pawnbroker loans, payday loans (most markets), high-APR loans (50%+), short-term loans repayable in 90 days or less, peer-to-peer lending (most markets).

**Cryptocurrency:** broadly restricted or requires special approval. Narrow exceptions exist (e.g. Argentina, Brazil, Bahrain) with licensing and TikTok sales-rep approval. 2026 added digital-currency advisory services in the UK and Ireland and crypto-mining devices in the US and Canada. Treat crypto as prohibited until someone confirms otherwise for the specific market.

**2026 carve-outs, each with sales-rep approval, a local licence and age targeting:** CFDs in the US and Canada; precious-metals trading in France, Italy, Germany and the UK; FX services in France; investment advice in Bolivia and Paraguay.

**Required disclosures where applicable:** interest rates, APR, transaction fees, repayment periods, representative examples, and relevant disclaimers. These need to be legible on screen — burying an APR in one-frame 12px text defeats the purpose and reads as deceptive.

**Prohibited claims:** deceptive or misleading claims about a product; omitting information on fees, charges, and tariffs; unrealistic expectations of effectiveness; exaggerated financial claims, including ambiguous "how to get rich" claims with no explanation of how; guarantees of winning; offers at unreasonably low prices designed to defraud.

Some markets (Cambodia) have no financial-ad restrictions; others (Albania, Myanmar, Uganda, and others) prohibit paid ads entirely.

## Alcohol

Allowed only in approved countries, with all necessary regulator licenses or lawful status in the target market.

- No targeting or appealing to people below the legal drinking age.
- **No featuring people under 25** on camera.
- No pregnant people.
- No portrayal of excessive drinking, intoxication, or reckless behavior under the influence.
- No offering alcohol as a prize or reward, and no offers or incentives encouraging consumption.
- Must clearly state alcoholic content and strength, and carry a responsible-drinking disclaimer on both ad content and landing page.

2026 additions: Cyprus and Luxembourg (June), alcohol-related venues in Cambodia, the Philippines, Singapore, Sri Lanka and Vietnam (July), and drinks above 5% ABV in Azerbaijan (August). National law can be stricter than TikTok. **Norway and Lithuania ban alcohol advertising outright**, and France's Loi Évin allows only a short list of factual content (see `references/law/eu-eea.md`).

## Gambling and Games

- Real-money gambling — online casinos, sports betting, any game of chance for money or stakes — requires TikTok's **certification process**, is allowed only in specified markets where gambling is legal, and only with all local certifications met.
- Age-appropriate targeting is mandatory; no gambling ads to minors, no ads directed at underage audiences, no featuring underage people.
- Surprise-based products — mystery boxes, loot boxes, purchases with guaranteed-but-unknown rewards — fall under restricted non-casino game rules.
- Game ads must not mislead with false offers. Gameplay footage that doesn't represent the actual game is a common and heavily enforced rejection; if the ad shows a puzzle mechanic, the game must contain that mechanic.

2026 changes:

- **Online casino** allowed since July 2026 in AT, BG, CH, CZ, DE, DK, ES, GR, HR, HU, IE, IL, LU, NL, PT, RO, SE, SI and SK.
- **Online gambling, fantasy sports and rewarded gaming** allowed in Kenya and Nigeria (July).
- **Rewarded gaming** allowed in Argentina and Guatemala, and **social casino** in Guatemala (June).
- **Brazil:** passive lotteries allowed (August), and then **all online gambling and gambling-information ads prohibited** (October).

TikTok allowing a market doesn't override national law. **Italy bans gambling advertising** (Decreto Dignità), and Spain, the Netherlands and Belgium restrict it heavily (see `references/law/eu-eea.md`).

## Prediction markets and event contracts

Allowed since July 2026 only in Canada, the US, the Philippines, Bolivia, Ecuador, Guatemala, the Dominican Republic, Paraguay, Colombia, Panama, Peru, the UK, Ireland, Denmark, Finland, Sweden and Israel. Each needs sales-rep approval, age targeting and clear disclaimers. Treat it as prohibited everywhere else. Where a product looks like betting on outcomes, local gambling law may apply as well.

## Adult Content and Dating

Prohibited in creative:

- Nudity; explicit exposure of breasts, buttocks, or genitalia.
- Depictions of sexual acts — penetrative, non-penetrative, or oral.
- Emphasis on sensitive body parts or regions; sexualized body positions; flirtatious movements; imitating sexual positions or acts; stripping or undressing.
- Swimsuits, lingerie, or clothing that exposes or implies exposure of intimate areas. (Narrow exception: swimwear ads and water-related products.)
- Excessive skin exposure; provocative body language.
- Sexually suggestive or explicit text or audio, including innuendo referencing specific sexual activity.
- Models appearing 21 years of age or younger in any of the above contexts.
- Promotion of infidelity.

**Dating:** apps designed purely for casual sex are not allowed. Sugar dating, or any companionship or relationship in exchange for compensation, is prohibited. Dating apps aren't allowed in every market. 2026 added Algeria, Bangladesh, Egypt, Pakistan, Turkey and the UAE (July), and live-chat apps in Pakistan and Bangladesh (June), each with sales-rep approval and age targeting. Confirm the user's market.

## Teen Safety and Wellbeing

- Products appealing to children under 13 must be **restricted to 18+** audiences in specified markets (US, UK, Canada, EU states, and others) and must avoid particularly childish creative approaches.
- Prohibited: minor sexual abuse material, real or AI-generated; physical abuse of minors (hitting, slapping, punching, kicking); emotional abuse (threatening behavior, humiliation, ridicule, bullying); inappropriate skin exposure that objectifies minors; minors drinking alcohol, gambling, or smoking.
- Prohibited: urging or pressuring teens to purchase, or encouraging teens to persuade parents or guardians to buy.
- Allowed with care: minors in swimsuits for swimwear and water-related ads; fictional depictions in film/TV trailers handled non-gratuitously; traditional artwork (restricted in Middle Eastern and African markets).

## Other restricted categories

Consult the live policy before producing in any of these — they carry market-specific certification requirements:

- **Politics, government, and elections** — political advertising is broadly prohibited on TikTok; government and state-affiliated media advertisers have separate rules. Since June 2026, landing pages may mention otherwise-prohibited content or people if that isn't their main focus.
- **Housing, Employment, and Credit (HEC)** — targeting restrictions apply in regulated markets; ads in these categories cannot use certain audience-narrowing options.
- **Animals and environment**, **violence and dangerous activities**, **suicide and self-harm**, **misinformation** — each has a dedicated policy page.
- **Hate speech and discrimination**, **bullying and harassment** — two separate policies since June 2026 (previously one "offensive content" policy).
- **Horoscopes and fortune-telling** — allowed only in specific markets (Bangladesh, Lebanon and Morocco added in July 2026).
- **Non-profits and NGOs**, **branded content** — market-specific requirements for approval; the NGO requirements changed in August 2026.
- **Pangle placements** carry their own separate industry-entry and creative policies.
