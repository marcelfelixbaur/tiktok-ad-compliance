# Creative and Format Rules

Visual and structural requirements: what can appear on screen, how the asset must be built, and what the landing page must contain.

## Production quality requirements

TikTok enforces a quality floor, and these are enumerated rules rather than guidance:

- **The ad image must not feature blurry, unclear, and unrecognizable visuals.** High resolution and legible.
- **The ad must contain audio and it must not be of poor quality**, such as unclear or muffled sound. A silent video is a rejection — this catches purely typographic and motion-graphic ads, which need at least a music bed or sound design.
- Standard aspect ratios: **9:16 vertical, 1:1 square, 16:9 horizontal.**
- Text must be legible — small type that survives on a desktop preview but not a phone fails.
- No spelling or grammatical errors in caption or on-screen text.
- Branding must be consistent between the ad and the landing page; an inconsistent product or brand identity across the two is a rejection.

Avoid black bars, letterboxing, and pillarboxing from mismatched source footage. Shoot or compose natively vertical rather than padding a 16:9 master.

## The motion requirement

Two related rules that shape how still-heavy and animated ads must be built:

- An ad **cannot rely on static images as its primary element** — static content may not exceed **50% of the video**.
- An ad **must incorporate interaction and cannot be motionless.**

A slideshow of product stills with a music bed is the textbook failure. Fixes that work: continuous camera movement (push-in, parallax on stills), animated typography carrying the message, live-action cutaways, or transitions that keep something moving through every beat. When animating from stills, keep a transform running rather than holding a frame.

## Prohibited on-screen content

Beyond the vertical-specific rules in `restricted-verticals.md`, these apply to all creative:

- **QR codes** in ad content (prohibited in select markets — treat as unusable by default and confirm per market).
- Nudity, sexually suggestive imagery, emphasis on sensitive body parts, sexualized positions, stripping, excessive skin exposure, lingerie or swimwear outside genuine swimwear advertising.
- Body shaming, ideal-body-type messaging, negative body imagery.
- Minors depicted in abuse, inappropriate exposure, or illegal activity; minors pressured to purchase.
- Violence and dangerous activities; suicide and self-harm content; shocking or gratuitous imagery.
- Discrimination, harassment, and bullying.
- Unauthorized third-party names, logos, or brands displayed in a way that could mislead about brand affiliation.
- Clips from unauthorized media sources used to promote a product.
- Personal data — a real person's contact details, credit card number, government ID, or similar.

Weapons *depicted* in TV drama, film, and cartoon scenes, and in video games, are generally allowed in ads for those works — the prohibition is on advertising weapons as products.

## TikTok brand assets and UI

Ad content and landing pages must not:

- Display elements related to TikTok — the TikTok logo, TikTok UI chrome, or descriptions like "TikTok Bestseller."
- Imply sponsorship, endorsement, or partnership with TikTok or ByteDance products without permission.

This is easy to violate accidentally: mocked-up "as seen on TikTok" badges, screen recordings that include the TikTok interface, and comment-section mockups all fail. A fake TikTok comment overlay is doubly bad — it's both unauthorized TikTok UI and a non-functional interactive element.

## Privacy and personal data

Ad creatives and landing pages must not disclose personal data, or state or imply that the product can be used in a way that infringes an individual's privacy. Showing a real person's contact details, credit card number, or government ID on screen is prohibited — including as set dressing in a demo.

Products framed around surveilling, tracking, or accessing another person's information without consent fall foul of this regardless of how the creative is shot.

## Landing page requirements

Reviewed alongside the creative; a fine ad pointing at a failing page is rejected.

- Functional, and working properly on the network of the targeted market.
- Not expired, erroneous, under construction, or incomplete.
- Mobile-friendly — works in portrait and landscape, text readable and buttons tappable without zooming.
- Sufficient original content, with a low ad-to-content ratio.
- Must not auto-download files.
- Must not require a download or personal information to access the main content.
- Must not require sensitive information — personal identification, financial or credit card information, health information, or biometric data — to access the site.
- Hashtag Challenge pages cannot be used as landing pages.
- Must not show restricted products even if those aren't what's being advertised. A game ad may not land on a page selling e-cigarettes.
- Consistent with the ad on product, price, discount, offer terms, and disclaimers.

**E-commerce landing pages** must display complete, accurate information: contact details (telephone, email, optional fax), company name, address, business license, pricing in local currency, shipping information, return and refund policy, terms, and privacy policy.

## Lead ads

- Require a clearly displayed privacy policy compliant with applicable data-protection regulation.
- Must not collect sensitive data — racial origin, political affiliation, health information, financial details, government IDs — without written consent from TikTok.
