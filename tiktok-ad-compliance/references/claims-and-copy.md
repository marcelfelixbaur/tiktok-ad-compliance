# Claims and Copy

Everything a viewer reads or hears: voiceover, on-screen text, ad caption, headline, display name, CTA, and landing-page copy. Most rejections that survive the industry gate die here.

## Misleading and false content

The governing rule: **ad content and landing pages must not promise or exaggerate results concerning a product's effect.**

Prohibited:

- **Promised or instant results** — "Get slim legs right away," "See results in 3 days," "Guaranteed to work."
- **Cures for incurable conditions**, and any medicinal claim to treat, cure, heal, or prevent a medical condition without the licensing to make it.
- **Absolute superiority** — "#1," "the best," "the only," "world's leading," "unmatched" — treated as misleading whether or not it's substantiated, unless the advertiser can evidence it and the market permits it.
- **Inconsistent information** between ad and landing page: mismatched product, price, discount, or offer terms, or disclaimers and terms present in one place and missing in the other.
- **Unrealistic expectations** of a product or service's effectiveness.
- **Exaggerated financial claims** — ambiguous "how to get rich" claims with no explanation of how, guarantees of winning, unreasonably low prices designed to defraud.
- **Omitted costs** — fees, charges, and tariffs must be disclosed, not buried.

Substantiation doesn't rescue a claim that TikTok categorically disallows. A supplement that genuinely helps some users still cannot say it cures anything.

## Deceptive practices

- Products or services that are or could be considered deceptive or misleading.
- Identity or personal-information theft via malware, spyware, or any unauthorized means.
- Document falsification, forgery, or counterfeiting.
- Distributing a high volume of identical or near-identical content across one or multiple accounts.

**Clickbait — the rule that catches animated and game ads:** *all interactive elements within an ad must function as intended, not to create a false or misleading experience.* Specifically prohibited:

- Fake play buttons
- Fake close / X / skip buttons
- Non-functional carousel indicators or progress bars
- Deliberately blocking or obscuring an image to force interaction
- Simulated system notifications, fake message alerts, fake battery or signal warnings

If a design instinct says "put a fake X in the corner so it feels native," that's the instinct to kill. The same applies to fake countdown timers and fake "1 spot left" counters — manufactured urgency that isn't real is misleading.

## Comparative and competitor claims

Restricted:

- Before-and-after comparisons that create a false impression. (Flatly prohibited for OTC medicines and supplements; extremely risky for any body-related product, where they also trip the body-image rules.)
- Malicious claims attacking competitors.
- Subjective negative judgments about a competing brand's attributes.

Comparison is not banned outright — a factual, substantiated, specific comparison can be fine. "Cheaper than every alternative" is not that; "£4 per serving vs. the £9 category average" is closer, if true and evidenced.

## Testimonials and endorsements

- Using a public figure's likeness without permission is prohibited, and especially so in a misleading context implying false endorsement.
- This extends to AI-generated or synthesized likenesses and voices of real people.
- Testimonials must not make claims the brand itself couldn't make. Putting "it cured my acne" in a customer's mouth does not launder the claim — review treats it as the advertiser's claim.
- User-generated and creator content used as an ad is still an ad: every rule here applies to the creator's spoken words.

## AI-generated and heavily edited content

Allowed, with disclosure. Per policy, significantly edited media and AIGC content is permitted if you **apply the AIGC label, or add a clear disclaimer, caption, watermark, or sticker.** Undisclosed AIGC risks rejection or restriction.

Read this broadly: AI voiceover, AI-generated b-roll, AI avatars, and synthetic product shots all count. Disclosure should be visible in the creative, not only in a campaign setting.

Separately: realistic synthetic depictions of real people require their permission, and synthetic minors are prohibited outright.

## Language quality

- **Ad caption and text must be free of spelling and grammatical mistakes.** This is an enumerated policy rule, not a style preference — typos get ads rejected.
- Copy must be in a language appropriate to the target market.
- No excessive symbols, and captions don't support clickable links, `@` mentions, or hashtags in non-Spark formats.
- Emojis are not supported in brand or app name fields; `{ }` and `#` are not supported in ad descriptions.
- Character limits are in `specs-and-safe-zones.md`.

## Rewrite patterns

The useful move is almost always shifting from outcome language to mechanism language.

| Rejected | Why | Compliant rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| "Lose 10lbs in 2 weeks, guaranteed" | Promised result, unrealistic weight loss, product-alone claim | "A 300-calorie meal replacement, ready in 60 seconds" |
| "The #1 skincare brand in the world" | Absolute superiority | "Over 40,000 five-star reviews" |
| "Cures your anxiety" | Medicinal claim | "Made with magnesium glycinate and L-theanine" |
| "Turn $100 into $10,000" | Exaggerated financial claim | "Commission-free trading. Capital at risk." |
| "You'll finally feel confident in your body" | Links appearance to life outcome | "Sizes 6–24, cut for a high waist" |
| Fake ⏸ button over the first frame | Clickbait / non-functional element | Real motion in the first frame; let the hook do the work |
| "Only 3 left!" (untrue) | Manufactured urgency | "Ships free through Sunday" (if true) |
